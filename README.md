# 🐧 Nesto

> **A community that helps you make a new city feel like home.**

Nesto is a community-driven platform designed primarily for students moving to unfamiliar cities for education.

Moving to a new city isn't just about finding a room.

You need to figure out **where to live, what to eat, who to trust, where to go, what to avoid, how much things actually cost, and how to feel at home in a place you've never lived before.**

Nesto brings this local knowledge together and organizes it into a useful, community-driven guide to the city.

---

# 🏠 The Problem

Every year, students move from smaller towns and cities to larger cities for colleges and universities.

One of the first challenges is finding suitable accommodation.

A place might look affordable and convenient online, but after living there for a few months, students may discover:

* Hidden costs
* Unreasonable rent
* Difficult landlords
* Poor living conditions
* Noise or lack of peace
* Bad connectivity to college
* Problems with food and nearby services
* Safety concerns
* Issues that weren't visible when they first moved in

By the time these problems become obvious, moving again can mean losing a security deposit, spending more money, and going through the entire process again.

But accommodation is only the beginning.

A student arriving in a new city also has to figure out:

* Where to eat
* Which tiffin services are reliable
* Where to spend an evening
* Which areas are convenient
* Where to study
* Which local services are useful
* Which places are safe
* What is actually popular among students
* What local residents know that isn't easily available online

The problem isn't simply a **lack of information**.

It is a **lack of organized, trustworthy local knowledge**.

Nesto aims to solve this by combining community participation with structured, city-specific information.

---

# 🌱 Our Idea

Nesto creates a **community for each city**, but it isn't simply a discussion forum.

The community is the **source of knowledge**, while Nesto organizes that knowledge into useful sections that newcomers can directly explore.

Think of it as asking:

> **"I'm new here. What should I know?"**

Instead of making the newcomer search through hundreds of posts and comments, Nesto can provide organized answers.

For example:

### 🏠 Rooms & Apartments

A dedicated accommodation section can show:

* Rooms suggested by the community
* Apartments and shared accommodations
* Typical rent in different areas
* Previous residents' experiences
* Landlord reviews
* Deposit information
* Hidden charges
* Distance from colleges
* Living conditions
* Community ratings and reviews

The community can suggest and review accommodations, while the platform organizes those recommendations into a useful list that newcomers can browse.

---

### 🍱 Food & Tiffin

Instead of simply asking:

> "Does anyone know a good tiffin service?"

Nesto can have a dedicated **Food & Tiffin** section.

It can contain community-reviewed:

* Tiffin services
* Messes
* Restaurants
* Affordable food spots
* Student-friendly places
* Local specialties

A newcomer can browse what the community has already discovered rather than starting from zero.

---

### 🌆 Things To Do

A city should not just be a place where you sleep and attend college.

Nesto can have dedicated sections for discovering the city.

For example:

**Best places to spend the evening**

**Best parks**

**Study spots**

**Cafés**

**Restaurants**

**Markets**

**Weekend destinations**

**Student hangouts**

**Hidden local gems**

These sections are built from community contributions, reviews, and experiences.

---

### 🛡️ Safety & Local Awareness

Nesto can also organize useful local information around safety and accessibility.

For example:

* Areas with good connectivity at night
* Nearby emergency services
* Community-reported safety concerns
* Places commonly avoided by residents
* Information particularly useful for students travelling at night
* Experiences from people who actually live in the area

Safety information must be handled carefully.

Nesto should prioritize **evidence-based information over rumors or fear**, with appropriate moderation, reporting, and verification mechanisms.

# 🧩 Community + Structured Information

This is one of the most important parts of Nesto.

Nesto has **two connected layers**.

### 1. 💬 Community

A normal community space where people can:

* Ask questions
* Start discussions
* Share experiences
* Give recommendations
* Help newcomers
* Discuss local issues
* Share useful information

This is the living, constantly changing part of Nesto.

### 2. 📚 City Guide

Useful information from the community can also become organized into dedicated categories.

For example:

> A community member recommends a particular tiffin service.

Other students review it.

The community verifies the information.

It becomes part of the city's **Tiffin Services** section.

Similarly:

> Someone recommends a particular room.

Other residents share their experiences.

The accommodation is reviewed and verified.

It can then appear in the **Rooms & Apartments** section.

The same model can work for restaurants, parks, study spots, evening hangouts, services, and other categories.

### **The community creates the knowledge.Nesto organizes the knowledge.**

This distinction is fundamental to the product.

Nesto should not force newcomers to dig through endless conversations to find useful information.

The goal is to turn community knowledge into something **discoverable, structured, and useful.**

---
# 🔄 How the City Guide Grows

The City Guide should not be a fixed list of categories decided entirely by the development team.

**Each city's guide should be able to grow organically from its own community.**

The community creates the information, while moderators help maintain its quality.

### 📝 Everything Starts With a Post

When a user creates a post, they can choose from the existing categories available in that city.

For example:

* 🏠 Rooms & Apartments
* 🍱 Food & Tiffin
* 🌳 Parks
* 📚 Study Spots
* 🌆 Evening Places

The user can then do one of two things:

### 1. ➕ Add a New Entity

The user can add something that does not already exist in that category.

For example:

> **Category:** Rooms & Apartments
> **Entity:** A particular room/apartment
> **Post:** Information, experience, price, location, photos, etc.

After moderation, that room can become an entity in the city's **Rooms & Apartments** section.

### 2. ⭐ Review an Existing Entity

If the entity already exists, the user can select it and create a post/review about it.

For example:

> **Category:** Rooms & Apartments
> **Entity:** Existing Room
> **Post:** Review of the room, landlord, rent, living conditions, etc.

This allows an entity to accumulate experiences from multiple members of the community instead of relying on a single person's opinion.

---

# 🆕 Creating New Categories

The system should also provide a **"New Category"** option.

This is important because we cannot predict every useful type of place or service that exists in every city.

For example, someone might discover that their city has a particularly popular:

> 🌅 **Sunset Points**

A user could:

1. Select **New Category**
2. Create the category **Sunset Points**
3. Add an entity, such as a specific sunset location
4. Write a post describing it
5. Submit the post for moderation

The proposed category and entity would then enter the moderation system.

If the moderators approve it, **Sunset Points becomes an official category in that city's City Guide**, and the submitted location becomes an entity within it.

---

# 🏙️ Every City Can Be Different

This means Nesto does **not** need every city to have exactly the same City Guide.

One city might have:

* Rooms & Apartments
* Tiffin Services
* Study Spots
* Evening Places

Another might additionally have:

* Sunset Points
* Cycling Routes
* Local Markets
* Trekking Spots

The categories are therefore **community-generated and city-specific**, while still being moderated to maintain quality.

This allows Nesto to adapt to the culture, geography, needs, and interests of each city instead of forcing every city into the same template.

---

# 🔁 The Core Flow

The entire process can be thought of as:

**User → Post → Category → Entity → Moderation → City Guide**

Or, when something completely new is discovered:

**User → New Category → New Entity → Moderation → New City Category → City Guide**

The important principle is:

> **The City Guide is not manually built by us.
> It is continuously built by the people who live there.**

Moderators act as the quality-control layer, not as the sole creators of the city's knowledge.

---

# 🧠 Why This Matters

This model allows Nesto to grow beyond the categories we initially imagine.

We might start with accommodation, food, study spots, and places to relax.

But once real communities begin using Nesto, people will discover needs and places that the development team could never have predicted.

The platform should therefore provide a structured way for **new knowledge to create new structure**.

That is what allows every city on Nesto to become its own evolving community guide.


# 🤝 A Community, Not Just an App

Nesto is built around one important idea:

> **The people who already live somewhere are the best source of local knowledge for the people who are about to arrive.**

A student who has spent six months in a neighborhood knows things that a search engine doesn't.

Someone who has already tried five tiffin services knows which ones are actually worth the money.

Someone who has rented a room knows whether the landlord's promises match reality.

Someone who has lived in an area knows what it is like after sunset.

Nesto connects that experience with the next person who needs it.

But instead of letting that knowledge disappear inside a comment section, Nesto can turn it into **organized community resources.**

---

# 🗳️ Community Governance

Nesto should not become another platform where a few anonymous administrators decide what everyone is allowed to see.

The long-term vision is a **community-governed platform**.

Possible mechanisms include:

* City-level moderators
* Community elections
* Moderator terms
* Reporting systems
* Evidence-based verification
* Reputation systems
* Transparent moderation decisions
* Community feedback

The goal is to keep the community healthy while preventing individual moderators from having unchecked control.

Moderators aren't supposed to **own** a city's information.

They are supposed to **help the community maintain it.**

---

# 🏆 Rewarding People Who Help

Helping someone find a good place to live can save them weeks of trouble.

Nesto can recognize those contributions.

A future reward system could allow users to:

* Thank people who helped them
* Give reputation points
* Earn community recognition
* Build trusted contributor profiles
* Receive badges for useful contributions

The goal isn't to turn everything into a game.

It is to encourage a culture where:

> **Helping the next student is worth something.**

* 🌐 A web application
